About this virtual pipe organ

A free online pipe organ simulator that runs in your browser, with no download or sign-up. You sit at a five-manual console: two stop jambs with more than 240 drawknobs between them, five manuals (Choir, Great, Swell, Solo and Echo) with thumb pistons, expression shoes, a crescendo pedal, toe studs and a 32-note AGO-compass pedalboard. Play with the mouse, a touch screen, the computer keyboard, or MIDI keyboards and pedalboards. Stops are modelled on famous organs: Haarlem St Bavo, Hamburg St Jacobi, Paris St-Sulpice and Notre-Dame, Liverpool Cathedral, Sydney Town Hall and Atlantic City.

Features: 25 couplers, sub and super octave couplers, tremulants, a 10-step crescendo pedal, sforzando, general and divisional pistons you can set yourself, a piston sequencer, adjustable rooms from studio to great cathedral, historic temperaments (Werckmeister, Kirnberger, Vallotti, meantone, Pythagorean) and Web MIDI input with single-keyboard and split modes. Each pipe is synthesised with its own tuning, speech and wind, so the sound is not a static electronic tone. It is built for current Chrome and Edge and also runs in Firefox and Safari; Web MIDI needs Chrome, Edge or Firefox.

How to play the organ: a quick registration guide

Stops and footage. Each drawknob brings on one rank of pipes. The number is the length of the lowest pipe: 8′ sounds at the written pitch, 16′ an octave lower, 4′ an octave higher and 2′ two octaves higher. 32′ stops, found here only in the Pedal, sound two octaves below written pitch and are felt as much as heard. Mutations such as the Nasard 2⅔′ and Tierce 1⅗′ add single harmonics; mixtures add several at once.

Building a hymn registration. Start the manual with an 8′ and a 4′ principal (Diapason), add a 2′ for brightness and a mixture for a full congregation. Give the Pedal a 16′ (Subbass or Open Diapason) and an 8′, then couple Great to Pedal so the bass follows your left hand. For a quiet verse, use flutes (Gedeckt, Rohrflöte) on the Swell with the box half closed. Reeds (Trumpet, Oboe) carry a melody on a separate manual over a soft accompaniment.

Couplers, pistons and the crescendo. Couplers let one keyboard play another division's stops: Swell to Great adds the Swell to the Great. Set a combination by pressing SET and then a piston; recall it later with one click or with MIDI program change. The crescendo pedal adds stops in ten steps from soft to full organ, and the sforzando gives full organ at once.

Learning hymns. Pick a hymn on the music desk. Listen plays it on the Great, Learn drops each note onto its key and waits for you, and Play keeps time and scores every note with stars, like a piano-learning game. The tunes are public-domain melodies taken from Hymnary.org.
